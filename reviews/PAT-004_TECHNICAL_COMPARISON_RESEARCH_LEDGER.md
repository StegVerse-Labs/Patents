## Purpose

This ledger defines bounded technical comparison tracks for later verified research. It contains no patentability opinion, novelty conclusion, legal prior-art result, medical conclusion, safety conclusion, or search-completeness claim.

## Working technical center

The structured family record describes a candidate combination involving:

```text
calibrated mutual-state contract
manifest-specified bounded node construction
transition-governed isolated and coupled operating states
separation of spontaneous activity from intentional communication
authorized coupling and disengagement

```

Implementation evidence is currently absent. Comparisons must therefore distinguish the structured proposal from verified implementation.

## Comparison tracks

### 1. Brain-computer and neural-interface systems

Investigate systems for passive sensing, decoding, communication, stimulation, closed-loop control, and bidirectional neural interfaces.

Comparison questions:

```text
Does the system distinguish passive observation, interpretation, communication, and stimulation authority?
Does it require explicit mutual calibration before coupling?
Does it preserve isolated autonomous states?
Does it retain reconstructable coupling and disengagement evidence?
```

### 2. Closed-loop neurostimulation and adaptive stimulation

Investigate adaptive stimulation systems that sense state and alter stimulation parameters.

Comparison questions:

```text
What state triggers stimulation?
How is authorization represented?
How are spontaneous and intentional states separated?
How does the system disengage or fail safe?
What retained evidence supports reconstruction?
```

### 3. Neural decoding and intention classification

Investigate classifiers that infer intent, movement, speech, communication, or cognitive state from measured signals.

Comparison questions:

```text
Does classification alone grant communication or action authority?
How is calibration performed and bounded?
How are uncertainty, non-inference, and ambiguous states handled?
Are state transitions receipted?
```

### 4. Mutual calibration and coupled-system protocols

Investigate protocols requiring reciprocal evidence, synchronization, negotiation, or shared-state establishment before joint operation.

Comparison questions:

```text
Is calibration unilateral or mutual?
Are isolated and coupled modes explicitly represented?
Can either participant revoke coupling?
How is recovery to autonomous operation demonstrated?
```

### 5. Manifest-governed and capability-bounded execution

Investigate machine-readable manifests, capability grants, policy-bound execution, and minimum-scope runtime construction.

Comparison questions:

```text
Does a manifest define addressability and authority?
Are processing components dynamically constructed?
Is execution bound to a transition state and expiry?
Does information admission remain separate from stimulation or action authority?
```

### 6. Safety interlocks and human-subject protection mechanisms

Investigate technical interlocks, emergency stop behavior, stimulation limits, fail-safe states, operator controls, and independent monitoring.

Comparison questions:

```text
What failures force disengagement?
What limits are technically enforced?
What authority can override or stop operation?
What evidence is retained after a failure?
```

This track is technical only. Regulatory, clinical, ethical, and legal conclusions require qualified specialists.

### 7. Event sourcing, provenance receipts, and reconstruction

Investigate systems that record state transitions, inputs, outputs, authority, integrity, and terminal state.

Comparison questions:

```text
Can the beginning, modification, and end of coupling be reconstructed?
Are receipt chains independently verifiable?
Are denied or failed transitions retained?
How are corrections and supersession represented?
```

### 8. Multi-entity observer-participant governance

Investigate protocols where an observer, operator, participant, or controller has distinct authority or evidence roles.

Comparison questions:

```text
Who observes neural state?
Who authorizes interpretation, communication, coupling, or stimulation?
Can observation affect the state under review?
How are lag, disagreement, and role transitions handled?
```
