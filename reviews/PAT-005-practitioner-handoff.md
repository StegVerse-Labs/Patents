## Questions for Counsel

1. Is the strongest filing strategy a single family, multiple families, or a core family plus continuation candidates?
2. Should publication-provenance claims remain separate from the physical-device continuity claims?
3. Which limitations provide the strongest technical distinction over device fingerprinting, IoT onboarding, digital twins, policy admission, and provenance systems?
4. Does the current written description sufficiently support canonical merge rules, ambiguity preservation, and reverse reconstruction?
5. Are destination response states best claimed as technical state-machine limitations, data-structure limitations, or both?
6. Which human contributors must be interviewed for each proposed claim?
7. What is the earliest enabling public disclosure date for each material limitation?
8. What foreign-filing options remain available?
9. Should any additional public disclosure be paused pending filing?
10. What formal drawing changes are required?

## Proposed Claim Tiers

### Tier A — Core physical-device continuity

Focus on observations, source-linked fingerprints, canonical identity, preserved ambiguity, destination classification, non-authorizing package, independent destination response, and reconstructable receipts.

### Tier B — Governance and authority state

Focus on the separation of observation, review, reliance, commitment, operation, refusal, and denial.

### Tier C — Cross-repository reconstruction

Focus on linked source, destination, guardian, mirror, publication, and admissibility records enabling reverse reconstruction.

### Tier D — Publication provenance

Evaluate separately because the field is crowded and may warrant dependent claims, a separate family, defensive publication, or exclusion.
