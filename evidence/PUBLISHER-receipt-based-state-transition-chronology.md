# Receipt-Based State Transition Validation — Chronology and Disclosure Intake

## Purpose

Collect attributable factual evidence without inferring conception, disclosure, priority, filing, or deadline dates.

## Events requiring evidence

For each event, record the date or date range, contributor, contribution, source path or stable identifier, repository and commit when applicable, confidentiality state, recipients or audience, and whether the record is contemporaneous or retrospective.

1. Earliest articulation of receipt-linked state transition validation.
2. Earliest pre-state, transition, post-state, and authority-binding formulation.
3. Earliest parent or predecessor receipt linkage.
4. Earliest stage-specific validation sequence.
5. Earliest explicit separation of readiness, publication, deployment, acceptance, and execution authority.
6. Earliest `ALLOW`, `DENY`, `FAIL_CLOSED`, or `QUARANTINE` receipt form.
7. Earliest chain-integrity validator or reconstruction routine.
8. Earliest representative fixture, test, runtime trace, or retained output.
9. Earliest internal disclosure to another person or organization.
10. Earliest public post, paper, repository release, demonstration, offer, sale, or unrestricted access.
11. Any correction, supersession, rollback, or materially expanded embodiment.

## Required source metadata

- exact file, message, issue, pull request, paper, or recording;
- immutable commit, blob SHA, export hash, or stable message identifier;
- author and recipients;
- creation and publication/access dates when independently supported;
- confidentiality or access restrictions;
- description of what the source actually teaches;
- relationship to later versions;
- known uncertainty or contradiction.

## Resumption condition

Automation resumes chronology normalization only after attributable source material is committed. Repository dates, draft dates, or conversation timestamps alone must not be converted into legal disclosure or priority conclusions.
