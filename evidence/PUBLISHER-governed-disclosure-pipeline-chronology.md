# Publisher Governed Disclosure Pipeline — Chronology and Disclosure Audit

## Purpose

This file is a factual intake surface for attributable, dated evidence. It does not establish conception, inventorship, ownership, public disclosure, priority, statutory consequences, or filing deadlines.

## Events requiring evidence

For each event, record the exact date or bounded date range, contributors, repository or document, immutable identifier where available, access conditions, and a concise description of the technical contribution.

1. Earliest governed disclosure-pipeline articulation.
2. Earliest separation of awareness, ingestion readiness, publication, activation, custody, execution, and admissibility.
3. Earliest declared-source and declared-destination validation.
4. Earliest canonical-hash or integrity validation.
5. Earliest refusal of incoming authority escalation.
6. Earliest exact blocker preservation and pending-state handling.
7. Earliest verification receipt explicitly separated from activation or closure authority.
8. Earliest publication-packet construction, redaction, secrecy classification, or claim-sensitive filtering.
9. Earliest generalized publication decision or authorization mechanism.
10. Earliest production closure receipt, custody record, reconstruction record, test, demonstration, or deployment.
11. Internal disclosures to collaborators, contractors, counsel, or prospective partners.
12. Public posts, papers, repositories, demonstrations, offers, sales activity, or unrestricted releases.
13. Later corrections, superseding versions, rollback behavior, or abandoned designs.

## Public-disclosure review questions

Record facts only:

- What exactly was accessible?
- On what concrete date did access begin?
- Was access restricted, confidential, private, or public?
- Was source code, architecture, a working demonstration, a paper, an offer, or only a high-level description exposed?
- Which candidate limitations were actually disclosed?
- Was the disclosure later corrected, removed, restricted, or superseded?

Legal consequences must be determined by qualified counsel.

## Resumption condition

Automation may normalize chronology and update readiness only after attributable records are supplied. Unknown dates remain unknown, and no deadline may be calculated from a repository commit, packet generation date, conversation timestamp, or undated recollection.
