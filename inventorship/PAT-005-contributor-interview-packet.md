# PAT-005 Contributor Interview Packet

**Purpose:** collect human conception evidence for claim-by-claim inventorship and chronology.  
**Status:** internal working record; not an inventorship determination.

## Interview Rules

- Interview each potential contributor separately before reconciling accounts.
- Ask for what the person conceived, not what they coded, owned, reviewed, or prompted.
- Preserve uncertainty and disagreement.
- Request contemporaneous evidence rather than relying only on memory.
- Do not suggest answers or convert repository authorship into inventorship.

## Open Narrative

1. Describe the technical problem as you understood it before the current implementation existed.
2. Describe the first solution you personally conceived.
3. What part of the solution was complete in your mind at that time?
4. What parts were supplied later by another person?
5. What alternatives did you consider and reject?

## Evidence Requests

Request copies or stable references for:

- dated notes, drawings, messages, issue discussions, or emails;
- earliest repository commits or local files;
- screenshots only when original machine-readable evidence is unavailable;
- test results showing the conceived behavior;
- public posts or presentations;
- contributor corrections to later descriptions.

## Collaboration Questions

1. Who first proposed separating package preparation from device-operation authority?
2. Who first proposed observation-only destination acceptance?
3. Who first proposed retaining source fingerprints after canonical merge?
4. Who first proposed reconstructing the path across source, destination, guardian, Site, Publisher, and wiki records?
5. Did anyone merely implement a fully formed idea supplied by another person?
6. Did AI-generated text contribute wording only, or did a human adopt and materially refine a technical concept?

## Confirmation

The interviewee should review a written summary and identify:

- factual errors;
- missing contributors;
- limitations they did not conceive;
- supporting evidence not yet collected;
- statements that remain uncertain.

No signature language should imply assignment, oath, declaration, or legal conclusion unless prepared by qualified counsel.
